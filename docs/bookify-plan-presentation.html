Part one

Payout Partner — the money layer.

What Payout Partner does

Money primitives, well-automated.

  • MoR invoicing from Payout Partner AS to the client, with gapless Norwegian invoice numbering.
  • Identity verification through BankID (via Criipto), with Folkeregister fallback.
  • BRREG checks on client organizations at creation and before each invoice.
  • Thursday lønn batch with arbeidsgiveravgift per municipality, skattekort-based withholding, and A-melding reporting to Skatteetaten.
  • Credit notes, reconciliation, MVA remittance — all bokføringsloven-compliant.
  • REST API (v2) so external integrators can build on top.
Who uses Payout Partner today

Three kinds of customers. All continue unchanged.

Direct freelancers

Sign up themselves at payoutpartner.com. Use it to invoice clients and get paid as lønn. Self-service.

B2B partners

Staffers, Luado, Leii, the anchor catering operator, and similar companies. Bring their own clients; use Partner Portal to record bookings, invoice, and pay freelancers.

API integrators

External platforms wanting Payout Partner as a payments backend. Consume the v2 REST API with a bearer token.

Where the model stops short

Payout Partner doesn't help anyone find each other.

It's a money layer. It assumes clients and freelancers already know each other, and just need the payroll handled.

That's the gap Bookify fills.

Part two

Bookify — the marketplace.

For Payout Partner people — the one-slide translation

Bookify is a thin marketplace layer over the pop-core invoicing you already know.

  • Bookify itself is a new account on Payout Partner. Owned by Payout Partner AS.
  • Shop owner = a freelancer enrolled on that account who also runs a public shop page.
  • Member = another freelancer enrolled on the same account.
  • Client = a standard pop-core Client record (BRREG-verified), with a marketplace membership granting access to private shops.
  • Job = one Invoice with per-line payees. Work lines route to the member; a commission line routes to the shop owner. The standard invoice state machine produces both lønn events.

Everything Bookify-specific is a thin marketplace layer on top of the invoicing you've been building for years. The hard work already happened in the pop-core primitives.

What Bookify is
“A Norwegian marketplace for hiring freelancers — with payments solved.” bookify.app · launching Phase 2
The shop owner

One person, one shop, forever.

  • You own a shop at bookify.app/nb/no/a/<your-slug>. Skill pages like /nb/no/kokker list every shop tagged with that skill.
  • You decide whether your shop is public (browsable, SEO-indexed) or private (gated by invitation).
  • You can invite roster members who perform jobs alongside you.
  • Your shop takes 5% on every job booked through it — paid to you as lønn, weekly.
  • You can pause or close the shop at any time.
The roster member

You do the work. You get paid.

  • Accept roster invitations from one or many shops.
  • See jobs proposed to you; accept or decline.
  • BankID verification once, via Payout Partner. Never again.
  • Paid as lønn through the Thursday batch once the client pays the invoice.
  • All Norwegian employer-tax obligations handled by Payout Partner AS. You get a clean payslip.
The client

You want work done. You want a proper invoice.

  • Norwegian business. Verified against BRREG on sign-up.
  • Submit a request on a shop page, or to up to five shops at once via the category page.
  • Get quotes. Accept one. Chat with the shop and assigned freelancer.
  • After the work is delivered, a Net-14 MoR invoice from Payout Partner AS arrives in your inbox. 25% MVA, fully compliant. The invoice explains that Payout Partner AS is the payments service for jobs booked on Bookify.
  • Pay by bank transfer with KID. That's it.
End-to-end

A job, step by step.

Proposal — for team discussion

A flat 100 kr Bookify fee per booking.

Today all the money flows to Payout Partner AS, the shop owner, and the freelancer. Bookify generates the demand but captures no marketplace margin. A flat fee per booking would change that — transparent on the invoice, same pattern as the MVA or commission line.

Tradeoffs to discuss

Pros. First real marketplace revenue line. Scales linearly with booking volume. Aligns incentives toward shipping a great bookify.app experience.

Cons. On a 600 kr minimum job the fee is 16.7% of the work value — disproportionate. Probably argues for a tiered or percentage-based model, or a minimum-job-size bump, rather than a flat 100 kr at every size.

Not in the current spec. No engineering commitment. Bringing this to the team to discuss the shape of a Bookify-side fee.

The rule everyone agrees to upfront

No client payment, no freelancer payment.

Payout Partner AS doesn't float the money. If a client doesn't pay, nobody gets paid.

Why it's safe: Norwegian B2B payment culture is predictable, and we only accept BRREG-verified clients.

A few hard numbers

The shape of a viable job.

Feedback window

48 hours after completion. Silence counts as confirmation.

Invoice terms

Net 14. No escrow, no inkasso.

Part three

How we built it.

The key architectural move

Commission is a line on the invoice, not a separate system.

One invoice per job. Work lines pay the member. A single commission line pays the shop owner. The existing invoice-to-payout state machine handles both.

No new payment hooks. No duplicate invoices. No percentage math at runtime. No rounding-policy decisions.

The full plan started with several more moving parts. We simplified it down to this single, boring primitive — which is the whole point.

Two brands, one codebase

Same Rails app, two domains, two stories.

payoutpartner.com

B2B infrastructure. Trusted, boring, reliable. The brand partners like Staffers, Luado, and Leii build on.

bookify.app

Consumer-facing marketplace. Owns the shop-quality story. Takes the reputational hits from marketplace incidents — protecting the infrastructure brand.

This is a deliberate brand separation. A management-facing pros-and-cons review on whether to eventually collapse into one brand is scheduled separately.

The legal picture

Two companies. One money flow. Clearly explained to every user.

  • Skiwo AS operates the Bookify product surface — the brand, the marketplace, the consumer-facing website at bookify.app. Skiwo AS owns Payout Partner AS 100% — a wholly-owned subsidiary.
  • Payout Partner AS provides the payment and payroll infrastructure. It is the merchant of record on every invoice (both on bookify.app and on payoutpartner.com).
  • Clients pay Payout Partner AS by bank transfer. Freelancers are paid by Payout Partner AS as frilanser lønn.
  • Bookify's website and every invoice explain this plainly: "Payout Partner AS handles payments and payroll for jobs booked on Bookify."
  • Per-brand ToS and privacy notices. Each domain shows its own operating entity clearly.
The engineering cost

One week to go live.

Building Bookify is cheap because almost everything already exists in Payout Partner. The launch is small; the hard part comes after.

Three Payout Partner additions

source_params JSONB for traceability. payee_freelance_profile_id on invoice lines. New commission line type. That's it.

Minimal Bookify module

bookify.app domain, a shop listing page, shop detail pages, a client order form, and the invoicing wiring. Ship it. Learn from it. Iterate.

Classic Payout Partner behaviour is unchanged for anyone not using these features. Existing partners (Staffers, Luado, Leii, and others) continue exactly as before.

Where the real effort lives

The hard part is traffic.

Once we launch, building more features is easy. The challenge is making Bookify a place clients actually come to hire, and freelancers actually come to list their services.

Supply side

Seed with the anchor catering operator. Invite existing Payout Partner freelancers who'd benefit from a marketplace presence. Open self-signup once the first flows work.

Demand side

SEO on skill and city pages is the long-term play. Paid acquisition and anchor-partner referrals bridge the gap while SEO warms up.

Product iteration

What we ship in week one is the minimum that works. Chat, disputes, public/private shop distinctions, shop lifecycle states — all added as use reveals which matter most.

Learning loop

Early jobs are the data. We watch what breaks, what friction shows up, and where clients drop off — and steer from there.

What Bookify deliberately excludes

Discipline about scope.

  • ENK/AS payouts — not now, not later. Bookify is lønn only. Operators who want ENK/AS use Payout Partner directly or go off-platform.
  • Card payments. Bank transfer only. Stripe Connect comes later (if ever).
  • B2C clients. Norwegian businesses with an organisasjonsnummer only.
  • Auto-assignment of jobs. Manual only. We want humans deciding.
  • Reviews, ratings, escrow, mobile apps, hours logging. Not in the first cut.
  • Language and interpretation services. Excluded from the 60 supported skills for now.
A deliberate product constraint

Bookify is lønn only. No ENK/AS.

Every freelancer on Bookify — member or shop owner — is paid as frilanser lønn through the Thursday batch. No ENK/AS invoicing path. No alternate payout arrangements. Ever.

Why lønn-only: one payment model. One money pipeline. No "is this person an ENK?" branching. Dramatically simpler to build, to support, and to explain.

What ENK/AS operators should do instead: use Payout Partner directly — the classic Partner Portal supports ENK/AS invoicing today. Or simply transact off-platform. Bookify doesn't police that; we just don't provide infrastructure for it.

What could go wrong

Risks we're watching.

Brand dilution

Marketplace-quality complaints could leak to the payroll brand. Mitigated by the two-brand separation, but still worth monitoring early on.

Supply/demand chicken-and-egg

Building a marketplace is hard. Starting with an anchor partner seeds the supply side; demand comes from SEO and category pages.

Partner confusion

Existing Payout Partner customers might wonder if we compete with them. Mitigated by keeping Partner Portal and Bookify strictly separate in the UI.

Regulatory drift

EU Digital Services Act, upcoming platform-work directive. Small surface today; monitor as scope grows.
